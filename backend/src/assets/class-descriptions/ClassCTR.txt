Class C Control Zone (CTR) is a type of controlled airspace typically established around airports with higher traffic volumes or complex airspace configurations.  It provides a higher level of air traffic control (ATC) service compared to Class D, including separation between all IFR and VFR flights, and requires all aircraft operating within it to be equipped with surveillance equipment (such as a transponder with ADS-B OUT) and maintain two-way radio communication with ATC.

Class D Control Zone (CTR) is a controlled airspace that surrounds a licensed aerodrome, typically extending laterally up to 6 to 8 nautical miles from the aerodrome and vertically up to 4,500 feet AGL (Above Ground Level), though exact dimensions vary by location and are shown on relevant Visual Terminal Charts (VTCs).
